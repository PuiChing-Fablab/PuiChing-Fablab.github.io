23–25 September 2026

GDTE 2026 — Humanoid Robot Soccer 3v3

Hangzhou Grand Convention and Exhibition Center
K1 Line Competition Soccer 3v3

2nd Place — PCMS-HRG-1 and 3rd Place — PCMS-HRG-2 in autonomous humanoid robot soccer 3v3.

From 23 to 25 September, PCMS-HRG was invited to compete in the 2026 Global Digital Trade Innovation Competition (Embodied Intelligent Robot Challenge) at Hangzhou Grand Convention and Exhibition Center. The two teams, PCMS-HRG-1 and PCMS-HRG-2, won second and third place respectively in humanoid robot soccer 3v3.

Robots had to pass, shoot and make attacking and defensive decisions autonomously, identify teammates' positions and coordinate in real time, with no human remote control. The event tested code, reliable robot operation, tactical design and multi-robot teamwork.

Pui Ching was the only secondary school invited to compete; the other entries came from universities and companies. Both school teams finished in the top three. PCMS-HRG-1 reached the final against a university team and took second place, while PCMS-HRG-2 finished third.

The delegation also entered humanoid and legged-robot shuttle-run events. Trying different challenges and exchanging ideas with university and industry teams gave students a broader view of embodied intelligence in practice.

22–26 August 2026

2nd World Humanoid Robot Games — Soccer 3v3 U19

National Speed Skating Oval (“Ice Ribbon”), Beijing
K1 Line Competition International Soccer 3v3 U19

Quarter-finals (Top 8) in Soccer 3v3 U19 at the World Humanoid Robot Games.

The second World Humanoid Robot Games brought 666 teams and 2,056 robots from 16 countries across six continents to Beijing to compete in 51 events. Pui Ching was invited to compete with two teams: PCMS-HRG in Soccer 3v3 U19 and FAMBOT in the Retail Service scenario event.

Soccer 3v3 U19 was new this year and set aside specifically for secondary school students. Robots must move, find the ball, choose between attacking and defending, and coordinate with teammates entirely on their own. PCMS-HRG kept its nerve through the group stage and reached the quarter-finals, adjusting robot settings and match strategy between games against far more experienced opposition.

In the parallel Retail Service scenario event, FAMBOT's robot had to pick products, restock shelves and patrol back to its station in sequence — a test of perception, navigation, grasping, system integration and sustained task execution. Sixty-six teams entered and only the top 16 advanced; competing as school students against corporate and university entries, the team held its own and gained rare experience under pressure. During the Games both teams were interviewed by Beijing Radio and Television Station.

20–23 August 2026

RCAP Asia-Pacific Beijing Masters — Humanoid OnStage U12

Beijing
K1 Line Competition International 1st Place

1st Place — RCAP CoSpace Humanoid OnStage U12.

The second RCAP Asia-Pacific Robot World Cup Beijing Masters drew 24 international teams from 16 countries and regions, with competitors from 28 countries and regions in total. Teams from the United States, South Korea and Russia entered for the first time, while Brazil and Australia fielded national squads assembled from several universities and RoboCup teams.

PCMS-HRG was invited to compete in the RCAP CoSpace Humanoid OnStage U12 individual event, which judges programming, motion choreography, creative expression and teamwork. Two team members split programming, robot operation, performance design and live presentation between them, testing motions and refining the show repeatedly in the run-up to the competition — and won the individual title.

Every junior team also had to join the Humanoid OnStage SuperTeam challenge, creating and performing a joint routine with teams from other regions. Working across languages and toolchains under time pressure turned out to be as instructive as the competition itself.

8–26 July 2026

Booster Champion Night Season 1 — 3v3 Simulated Soccer

Online
K1 Line Competition Online Qualified

Cleared the qualifying round — PCMS-HRG beat the AI benchmark team to pass qualification for Season 1 of the 3v3 championship.

Between the RoboCup finals and the summer competitions in Beijing, the team was invited to compete in Booster Robotics' first Champion Night season — a 3v3 simulated soccer league played entirely in Booster Studio, with code submitted for automated matches.

Simulation strips away hardware failures and battery limits and leaves nothing but strategy: behaviour-tree design, role assignment, positioning and ball pursuit. It is the cheapest place to fail, and the fastest place to iterate, so improvements found here fed straight back into the physical robots' match code.

RoboCup 2026 — Humanoid Soccer League, Incheon

Songdo Convensia, Incheon, South Korea
T1 Line Competition International 5th in the world

5th in the world — RoboCup Humanoid Soccer League, Large Division (Major bracket).

PCMS-HRG was invited to compete in the RoboCup Humanoid Soccer League (HSL), Large Division, in the Major bracket alongside university and research teams. The event gave students experience with humanoid robotics at a high international level.

The competition brings together robot hardware, gait control, visual recognition, real-time decisions and teamwork. Over five intensive days, students adjusted software and hardware, tested strategies and responded to changing match conditions.

PCMS-HRG reached the quarter-finals before being eliminated, finishing 5th in the world in its first appearance at this level.

19 April 2026

Beijing Yizhuang Humanoid Robot Half Marathon

Yizhuang, Beijing
K1 Line Competition International Joint teams

The 2026 Beijing Yizhuang Half Marathon and Humanoid Robot Half Marathon put robots and human runners on the same 21.0975 km course — over a hundred teams, 105 humanoid robots, five international entries and some 12,000 human runners. Organisers announced they would submit the event for a Guinness World Record for the most autonomous humanoid robots in a half marathon.

Pui Ching was the only secondary school in the world invited to compete, through two joint teams built with Macau University of Science and Technology and international partners:

  • Portugal–Macau joint team (No. 065), with Instituto Superior Técnico, Lisbon — running the Tien Kung Ultra-2026 humanoid (approx. 175 cm, 75 kg), tuned for gait stability, pace control and wet or sloped road surfaces.
  • Brazil–Macau joint team (No. 068), with Brazilian education and research partners — running the Booster K1 (0.95 m, 19.5 kg), with upgraded real-time gait planning and adaptive compliance control to hold posture as joints heat up over distance.

Thirteen students from Primary 3 through Senior 1 supported the entry, handling event documentation, technical study and team preparation. An open road over 21 km is a far harsher test than any lab: battery endurance, thermal management, terrain adaptation and fall recovery all had to work at once, for hours.

11–12 April 2026

Humanoid Half Marathon — Full-Process Night Test

Yizhuang, Beijing
K1 Line Test event Preparation

A week before the race, both joint teams gathered in Yizhuang for the official full-process, full-element test — run overnight from the evening of 11 April into the early hours of 12 April, to competition standard. Course transit, scheduling, equipment coordination and emergency response were all rehearsed exactly as they would run on race day.

More than seventy teams attended, including several international entries. For the students, watching a robot cover real distance on real asphalt at night made the gap between laboratory testing and open-road operation immediate and concrete.

7–12 April 2026

1st Beijing Middle School Humanoid Robot Soccer League

Capital Normal University Affiliated Science City School, Haidian, Beijing
K1 Line Competition Invited team 5-a-side

The first Beijing (Haidian) Middle School Humanoid Robot Soccer League was an embodied-AI proving ground built specifically for secondary school students. No remote control and no outside intervention are permitted — results come purely from student-written code and how the robots behave on the day.

The competition included eligibility checks, qualifiers, a group stage and finals. Each team fields five humanoid robots at once, following referee commands for kick-off, attack-defence transitions, substitutions and penalties.

Before each match, students checked software versions, configuration, network connections, cameras and logs, then tested localisation and changed batteries. During play, they diagnosed falls, localisation errors and module faults under the competition procedures. Each match put programming, system debugging, practical engineering and teamwork to the test.

February 2026

Beijing Training Camp — Middle School Humanoid Soccer League

Beijing
K1 Line Training Invited by Booster Robotics

Booster Robotics invited PCMS-HRG to Beijing for the training and invitational matches attached to the first Middle School Humanoid Robot Soccer League — recognition of the school's work in humanoid robot development and robotics education, and a rare chance for students to learn, build and demonstrate at this level.

Booster K1 robots were provided for the duration. Training ran systematically through the full competition stack: hardware structure and safe handling, development environment and ROS 2 deployment, YOLO inference and visualisation, behaviour-tree decision-making with striker and goalkeeper strategies, then multi-robot communication and cooperative match play — building step by step toward the ability to run a complete humanoid soccer match independently.

The camp gathered leading secondary school teams including the high schools affiliated to Tsinghua University, Peking University and Renmin University, and Beijing No. 101 Middle School, giving students the chance to measure themselves against strong opposition and learn from them directly.
