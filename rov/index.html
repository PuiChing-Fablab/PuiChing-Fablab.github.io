02

The work

A vehicle goes from a blank sheet to a competition tank without ever leaving the students’ hands — and at MATE it has to arrive with a company built around it.

Done in-house Structural design · 3D modelling and printing · PCB layout · material selection · waterproofing · machining · control software · tuning · piloting

The underwater robotics team at Fablab Pui Ching (Macau) is run by junior secondary students who take a vehicle all the way from a blank sheet to a competition tank. Structural design, 3D modelling and printing, PCB layout, material selection, waterproofing, machining, control software, tuning and piloting — every part of it is done by the students themselves.

At MATE the team does not simply enter a robot. Alongside the vehicle it has to produce a full technical documentation package, manage a project budget, design its marketing material, and defend the engineering in English before a panel of working ocean engineers.

“It operates as a start-up company — budget, documentation, marketing, and an English defence before working ocean engineers.”
Which is why the Engineering Presentation finished 2nd in the world in 2026

2026 SeaPerch theme Storm Response — technology in disaster recovery and rescue

Every competition task the team trains for is drawn from real subsea work: mapping and conserving coral, predicting iceberg drift, installing and maintaining offshore power equipment, deploying hydrophones, terminating cables on seabed instruments, and collecting data under ice.

So the engineering is pointed somewhere. The team built a computer-vision model to recognise invasive species, and carries its water-quality instrumentation on a surface platform of its own design. The goal is a practical contribution to marine conservation and to sustainable water resource management — made by students, from Macau.

Fleet register

Different competitions call for very different vehicles: a Ranger-class ROV for MATE, a compact SeaPerch platform for the RoboNation circuit, a manipulator-heavy build for water-operations events, and a surface craft carrying its own water-quality payload.

MATE Ranger class

Mosasaurus

The team's flagship ROV, rebuilt for the 2026 season. It carries a 360°-rotating manipulator and 3D-printed task tooling designed against the mission list, and runs a YOLO vision model for identifying invasive species.

  • Manipulator360° rotating

SeaPerch platform

Delta series

Small, fast and hand-built for the SeaPerch obstacle and mission courses. The Delta boats are where the youngest members learn buoyancy trim, thruster layout and clean piloting under a clock.

Water-operations build

Task vehicle

Built for the China Robot Competition's underwater operations event: releasing and installing hydrophones, torquing seabed instrument connectors, terminating cables to the millimetre and throwing switches on the bottom.

Survey platform

A surface craft for ecosystem surveillance. Its surveillance system was redesigned alongside the new hull, keeping the two loosely coupled so they integrate tightly without getting tangled together.

University of Maryland, USA · 30–31 May 2026 · organised by RoboNation · 210 teams worldwide

MATE ROV Competition World Championship

St. John's, Canada · 25–27 June 2026 · 24th edition · 86 teams from 16 countries and regions

Representing Macau, Team Mosasaurus finished 9th in the world among Ranger-class teams, and second in the world in the Engineering Presentation — 3.6 points off first place. Running as a start-up company, the team completed coral ecosystem mapping, subsea observatory maintenance and under-ice data collection in cold water, while producing the technical report, managing the budget, designing the poster and defending all of it in English.

China Robot Competition & RoboCup China Open — Underwater Robot / Water Operations, University Division

North China University of Technology, Beijing · 21–23 Aug 2026 · hosted by the Chinese Association of Automation

Competing a division up against top university teams from across the country, the students took First Prize. The event is themed on marine survey and extreme subsea work: within a time limit the vehicle had to release and install a hydrophone, screw connectors onto a seabed instrument and terminate its cable precisely, throw switches, and handle loose parts on the bottom.

Stn 06 2026.04 Runner-up The team also ran the competition’s Fun Day, lending its ROV to the public to pilot.

IET/MATE ROV Competition — Hong Kong Regional

Tsing Yi, Hong Kong · 25–26 Apr 2026 · organised by the IET · qualifier for the World Championship

The mission set was built from live ocean engineering problems: iceberg drift prediction, coral conservation research, and the installation and maintenance of offshore power equipment. Team Mosasaurus answered them with a rebuilt vehicle — new 3D-printed tooling, a 360°-rotating manipulator, and a YOLO model for recognising invasive species. The team also lent its ROV to the public at the competition's Fun Day, letting visitors pilot it and explaining how it works.
